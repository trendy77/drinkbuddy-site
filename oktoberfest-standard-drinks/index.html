Oktoberfest standard drinks: how many are really in a stein?

Oktoberfest 2026 runs from 19 September to 4 October, and whether you're at Theresienwiese in Munich or a marquee closer to home, the beer arrives the same way: a full litre in a glass mug. That stein looks like one big beer. In Australian standard-drink terms, it's nearly five. Here's the maths — and how to enjoy a long day at the tents without it getting away from you.

Why a stein is bigger than it looks

Two things make an Oktoberfest beer add up fast. First, the serve: beer at the festival is sold by the Maß, a one-litre mug — more than two-and-a-half of the schooners you'd get at an Aussie pub, in a single glass. Second, the strength. Munich Festbier (the official Oktoberfestbier) is a stronger golden lager, generally between 5.8% and 6.3% ABV, against roughly 4.8% for a typical Australian full-strength beer.

Put a bigger glass and a stronger beer together and the gap with a normal beer is wide. It doesn't feel like five drinks going down — it feels like one, in good company, with a band playing. That mismatch between how much you've had and how much it feels like is exactly what a quick sense of the numbers fixes.

The Oktoberfest standard-drink maths

An Australian standard drink is 10 grams of pure alcohol — roughly what your body clears in an hour. You can work any drink out with a simple formula the Australian Government's standard drinks guide uses: litres × alcohol percentage × 0.789. For a one-litre stein at 6%, that's 1 × 6 × 0.789 ≈ 4.7 standard drinks. Here's how the common Oktoberfest serves compare:

Oktoberfest serveStandard drinks
Maß / stein (1 litre Festbier, 6%)4.7
Maß at the strong end (1 litre, 6.3%)5.0
Half Maß / Halbe (500ml, 6%)2.4
Weissbier / wheat beer (500ml, 5.4%)2.1
Radler (1 litre, beer + lemonade, ~2.5%)2.0
For comparison: Aussie schooner (425ml, 4.8%)1.6

So a single stein is about the same as three schooners at once — and it's already at or above the four-drinks-in-a-day ceiling in Australia's NHMRC alcohol guidelines, which recommend no more than 10 standard drinks a week and no more than 4 on any one day to keep the risk from alcohol low. Two steins is roughly nine to ten standard drinks — close to the whole weekly ceiling in one afternoon. If you want the full breakdown by beer size and strength, our guide to standard drinks in a beer lays it out.

One mug, a whole day's ceiling. Because a Maß is nearly five standard drinks, you reach the NHMRC single-day limit before you finish your first beer. That's not a reason to panic — it's a reason to make each one last, and to know that "just a couple of steins" is a genuinely big session.

A pacing plan for the tents

Before you sit down

  • Sort how you'll get back first. Munich's U-Bahn and S-Bahn run straight to Theresienwiese, so there's rarely any need to drive. Decide on public transport, a taxi or a walk before the first beer, and driving is simply off the table.
  • Pick a number. Choose a realistic total for the day and stick to it. Counting in steins helps: two Maß is already a very big day by any Australian measure.
  • Eat first, and keep eating. The tents serve proper meals for a reason. Food in your stomach slows how fast alcohol is absorbed and flattens the early spike — order the pretzel, the roast chicken, the whole plate.

Through the day

  • Make each stein last. Your body clears about one standard drink an hour, and a Maß holds nearly five — so nursing one over an hour or two still keeps you climbing, not level. Slow is the whole game here.
  • Order a Halbe or a Radler. A half-litre halves the alcohol of a full stein, and a Radler — beer cut with lemonade — is weaker again. Both let you keep a drink in your hand while your count barely moves.
  • Alternate with water. Tap water (Leitungswasser) and soft drinks are available; one between every beer roughly halves your count over the day and takes the edge off tomorrow. healthdirect's safe-drinking tips put this near the top of the list.
  • Ease off before the end. Switch to water or a soft drink an hour or two before you leave. You'll navigate the crowds and the train home far better for it.
Travelling for it? If you're heading to Munich, keep an eye on your belongings in the crush, agree on a meeting spot with your group in case phones die, and read the Australian Government's Smartraveller advice for Germany before you go. A big day is a lot more fun when the getting-home part is already sorted.

Getting home — and the morning after

Plan not to drive at all. Germany's blood-alcohol limit is 0.05 — the same as a full Australian licence — and after a stein or two you'll be well over it for hours. Public transport is the easy answer at Oktoberfest, and it's why so few people drive to it in the first place.

The trap people miss is the next morning. Alcohol clears at a fixed rate of about one standard drink an hour, and nothing — coffee, a big breakfast, a cold shower — speeds it up. Finish a nine- or ten-standard-drink day in the evening and you can still be over the limit well into the next day. If you're driving anywhere the morning after, read our guide to driving the morning after drinking first.

Estimates are estimates. BAC calculators — including Drink Buddy's — give a guide based on averages, not a measurement. Your real blood alcohol depends on your body, food, medication and more. If there's any doubt at all, don't drive.

Enjoy the whole festival

None of this is about skipping the steins. Oktoberfest is a once-a-year occasion, and a Maß in a packed tent with a band going is part of the point. It's about staying good company from the first beer to the last train, remembering the day, and not turning a great festival into a rough one. Knowing a stein is nearly five standard drinks is what lets you pace it — the same trick that keeps a music festival or a day at the footy under control.

If you'd rather not do the maths in a noisy tent, log each stein as it lands. Seeing your standard-drink count tick up in real time is the nudge no amount of good intentions can match.

Frequently asked questions

How many standard drinks are in an Oktoberfest stein?

A Maß is a full litre of Festbier at around 6% ABV, which works out to about 4.7 Australian standard drinks — and closer to 5 at the strong end. That means a single stein already meets or exceeds the four-a-day ceiling in the NHMRC guidelines.

How strong is Oktoberfest beer?

Munich Festbier usually runs between 5.8% and 6.3% ABV, noticeably stronger than the 4.8% of a typical Australian full-strength beer. That extra strength, plus the one-litre serve, is why a stein carries so much more alcohol than it looks.

How many steins can I have and stay under the limit?

Even one Maß meets or exceeds the NHMRC single-day ceiling of four standard drinks, and two is roughly nine to ten — about a whole week's ceiling in an afternoon. There's no risk-free number; pace it slowly, alternate with water and eat as you go.

Can I drive after drinking at Oktoberfest?

Plan not to. Germany's limit is 0.05, and your body clears only about one standard drink an hour, so a stein or two keeps you over for many hours. Use public transport, and remember nothing but time lowers your BAC — the same goes for the morning after.

How do I pace myself at Oktoberfest?

Treat each stein as what it is — nearly five standard drinks — and make it last. Eat a proper meal, drink water between beers, order a half-litre or a Radler when you can, and set a limit before you sit down rather than in the moment.
