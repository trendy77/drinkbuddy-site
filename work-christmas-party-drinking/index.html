How to pace yourself at the work Christmas party

Free bar, finger food, and a room full of people you'll see again in January: the work Christmas party is designed to catch you out. Here's how to enjoy the night, stay on the right side of embarrassing, and get home safely in the middle of the silly season.

Why the work party catches people out

Most nights out have two natural brakes on your drinking: the cost of each round and the walk to the bar. A work Christmas party removes both. When the drinks are free, or on a company tab, there's no running total ticking over in your head — and when waiters bring trays to you, you never have to decide to get another.

Then there are the nerves. A lot of people arrive at the work party a little anxious — about small talk, about the boss, about dancing — and the quickest-feeling fix is a fast first drink or three on an empty stomach. That's exactly the combination that turns a fun night into the story people whisper about at the Monday stand-up.

The stakes are higher here, too. These are colleagues, not mates who've seen you at your worst, and one over-the-top night can shadow you into the new year — so a little planning goes a long way.

The open-bar maths

Pacing starts with knowing what you're actually holding. An Australian standard drink is 10 grams of pure alcohol — roughly what your body clears in an hour — and, as the Australian Government's standard drinks guide shows, almost nothing poured at a party equals exactly one:

Party serveStandard drinks
Glass of wine (150ml, 13%)1.5
Glass of sparkling (150ml, 12%)1.4
Schooner of full-strength beer (425ml, 4.8%)1.6
Stubby of full-strength beer (375ml, 4.8%)1.4
Nip of spirits in a mixer (30ml, 40%)1.0
Can of mid-strength beer (375ml, 3.5%)1.0

So "a couple of wines and a few beers" — say five drinks by feel — is realistically seven or eight standard drinks. That's nearly a whole week's worth in one evening, well past the NHMRC guideline of no more than four standard drinks on any single day. Bubbles are the sneakiest of the lot, because the glasses are small, the drink goes down easily and the top-ups never stop — our guide to standard drinks in a glass of champagne breaks down just how fast they add up.

A pacing plan that keeps your reputation intact

Before you go

  • Eat a proper meal. Food in your stomach slows how quickly alcohol is absorbed, which flattens the early spike from drinking on arrival. Canapés and a handful of chips don't count — eat before you leave.
  • Pick your number. Decide your total for the night before you walk in, and make it realistic. A limit chosen sober at 6pm survives; one improvised at 10pm at the bar does not.
  • Sort your ride home now. Book the rideshare, check the last train, or line up a lift before you've had a drink — it's the single most important decision of the night, and the hardest to make well once you're three drinks in.

At the party

  • Start with something soft. Walk in and get a water or a soft drink first. It settles the nerves without starting the clock, and means your first alcoholic drink isn't hitting an empty, anxious system.
  • One an hour, tops. Your body clears roughly one standard drink an hour, so this ceiling keeps you close to level rather than climbing all night. Remember most serves are 1.4–1.6, so "one an hour" means genuinely sipping it across the hour.
  • Alternate with water — every time. A glass of water between every alcoholic drink roughly halves your count over the night and softens the hangover. healthdirect's safe-drinking tips put this near the top of the list for good reason.
  • Dodge the rounds and shots. A round of shots is one to two standard drinks in ten seconds with no brakes — and the office shout is how a good night quietly gets away from you. It's fine to sit one out.
  • Beat the top-up. Keep your glass at least half full when you're pacing — staff and enthusiastic colleagues refill the emptiest glasses first — or just hold a full one you're not racing through.
  • Downshift after dinner. Switch to mid-strength beer, a spritz, or a soft drink in a nice glass once the formal part is done. By the time the dance floor opens, nobody knows or cares what's in your hand.
Know your exit. Decide in advance roughly when you'll leave — and actually go. The last hour of a free bar is where most regrets are made. Leaving while you're still having a good time is a genuine skill, and the people who master it are the ones who never become a Christmas-party cautionary tale.

Getting home — and the silly-season roads

Plan not to drive. Even careful pacing across an evening can leave your blood alcohol concentration above 0.05, and no app, chart or rule of thumb can tell you you're safe to drive — including ours. Book a rideshare before the rush, check the last train, or share a taxi with colleagues heading your way.

The festive season is the worst possible time to risk it. Police run extra random breath testing through December, and in NSW the double demerit period runs across Christmas and New Year — doubling the points for speeding, mobile phone, seatbelt and helmet offences. Drink driving isn't part of the double-demerit scheme because its penalties are already severe every day of the year: a mid-range reading can mean losing your licence, a heavy fine and a criminal record. Losing your licence in January is a hard way to start a year.

The trap people forget is the next morning. Alcohol clears at a fixed rate of about one standard drink an hour, and nothing — coffee, a big breakfast, a cold shower — speeds it up. Knock off at 1am after a solid night and you can still be over the limit at 9am. Before you drive anywhere the morning after, check our guide to how long it takes to get back to zero.

Estimates are estimates. BAC calculators — including Drink Buddy's — give a guide based on averages, not a measurement. Your actual blood alcohol depends on your body, food, medication and more. If there's any doubt at all, don't drive.

Enjoying the party is the point

None of this is about being the person nursing a lemonade in the corner. It's about being present for the night, remembering the good bits, and walking into work in the new year with your reputation exactly where you left it. The best version of the work Christmas party is the one you actually enjoy — and still feel fine about on Monday.

Frequently asked questions

How many standard drinks is a glass of wine at the Christmas party?

A 150ml pour of wine at 13% is about 1.5 standard drinks, and a glass of sparkling at 12% is about 1.4. Venue glasses are often bigger than 150ml and get topped up early, so most people drink more than they count.

Is it okay not to drink at the work Christmas party?

Completely. Hold a soda and lime, a mid-strength beer or an alcohol-free drink and you'll get no questions — a full glass in your hand ends the topic before it starts.

Can I drive home from the work Christmas party?

Plan not to. Your body clears only about one standard drink an hour, so even a few drinks can leave you over 0.05, and you can still be over the limit the next morning. Sort a rideshare, lift or public transport before you go.

How do I avoid getting too drunk in front of colleagues?

Eat beforehand, decide your limit before you arrive, alternate every drink with water, skip the shots and rounds, and switch to something lighter after dinner. Tracking your count removes the guesswork an open bar is built to create.

Do double demerit points apply to drink driving over Christmas?

In NSW, festive double demerits apply to speeding, mobile phone, seatbelt and helmet offences — not drink driving, which carries heavy penalties including licence loss all year round. Police also run extra breath testing over the silly season, so the safest plan is simply not to drive after drinking.
