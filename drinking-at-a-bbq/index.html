Drinking at a BBQ: how many standard drinks, and how to pace yourself

By Trendy Publishing editorial team · Published 3 September 2026 · Sources

Spring is BBQ season, and with Father's Day landing in early September the backyard get-together is back on. It's the most relaxed drinking there is — no last drinks, no queue, just a chair in the sun and a full esky. That's exactly why it sneaks up on people. Here's how to enjoy the whole afternoon and still get home the easy way.

Why the backyard catches people out

A pub or a footy ground does some of the pacing for you: you queue, you pay, and eventually they call last drinks. A BBQ has none of that. The drinks are self-serve, the esky is an arm's reach away, someone keeps topping up your glass before it's empty, and the whole thing runs for five or six hours. There's no natural cue to tell you where you're up to.

Add a warm spring day and a steady graze of snags and salads, and it feels like you've barely had a drink — right up until you stand up. The problem isn't that anyone sets out to overdo it. It's that casual, all-day, self-poured drinking is genuinely hard to keep track of. That's where a quick sense of the maths helps.

The backyard standard-drink maths

An Australian standard drink is 10 grams of pure alcohol — roughly what your body clears in an hour. As the Australian Government's standard drinks guide shows, almost nothing you pour at a BBQ equals exactly one — and home pours are usually bigger than a bar would give you:

Backyard serveStandard drinks
Light beer (375ml stubby/can, 2.7%)0.8
Mid-strength beer (375ml stubby/can, 3.5%)1.0
Full-strength beer (375ml stubby/can, 4.8%)1.4
Longneck / tallie (640ml, 4.8%)2.4
Jug of full-strength beer (1140ml, 4.8%)4.3
Generous glass of wine (150ml, 13%)1.5
Big pour of wine (200ml, 13%)2.1
Glass of cask wine (150ml, 12.5%)1.5
Home spirit, free-poured (60ml, 40%)1.9
Pre-mix can (375ml, 6%)1.8

So a relaxed "four or five beers" over the afternoon — if they're full-strength stubbies — is already six to seven standard drinks. Switch to a few generous wines and you're higher again. Either way, that's above the four-drinks-in-a-day ceiling in Australia's NHMRC alcohol guidelines, which recommend no more than 10 standard drinks a week and no more than 4 on any one day to keep the risk from alcohol low. If you want the full rundown by can and bottle size, see our guide to standard drinks in a beer.

The free pour is the trap. At home there's no jigger and no standard glass. A "glass of wine" is whatever fills the glass, and a spirit poured by eye is often nearly two standard drinks, not one. When you pour your own, the count climbs faster than it feels like it should.

A BBQ pacing plan

Before you arrive

  • Sort your trip home first. Deciding how you'll get home before the first drink is the most important call of the day. If you're driving, that decision is made — you're not drinking (more below).
  • Pick a number. Choose a realistic total for the afternoon and stick to it. A limit set on the way there survives; one improvised at 4pm doesn't.
  • Bring what you'll actually want. Pack some mid-strength or light beer, and something non-alcoholic you'll enjoy, so slowing down isn't a downgrade.

Through the afternoon

  • Eat while you drink. A BBQ is one occasion where the food is right there — use it. Food in your stomach slows how fast alcohol is absorbed and flattens the early spike. Graze steadily rather than drinking through to a late feed.
  • One an hour, at most. Your body clears about one standard drink an hour, so this keeps you roughly level instead of climbing all day. Remember most backyard pours are 1.4 to 2 standard drinks each, so "one an hour" means making it last.
  • Alternate with water. One water or soft drink between every drink roughly halves your count over the day and takes the edge off tomorrow. healthdirect's safe-drinking tips put this near the top of the list for good reason.
  • Pour your own, and pour smaller. It's the only way to know what you've actually had. Wave off the top-up until your glass is empty, so one drink doesn't quietly become three.
  • Go mid-strength. Swapping full-strength stubbies for 3.5% mid-strength drops each one from about 1.4 standard drinks to 1.0 — so you can keep a cold one in your hand all afternoon while your count barely moves.
  • Ease off early. Switch to water or a soft drink an hour or two before you head off. It gives your body a head start and you'll feel far better tonight.
Hosting? You set the pace for everyone. Put out plenty of water and a few good non-alcoholic options, serve the food early and often, and don't rush to refill empty glasses. A jug of beer on the table pours out to more than four standard drinks — handing out stubbies makes it far easier for everyone to keep count.

Drinking in the sun

Alcohol is a diuretic, and a warm spring or summer day makes you sweat, so a daytime BBQ dehydrates you faster than an evening indoors. You feel the effects sooner and tend to drink quicker to stay cool. Dehydration doesn't actually raise your blood alcohol — but it does make the next-day hangover worse, so drinking water alongside and finding some shade helps on both counts. Getting home — and the morning after

Plan not to drive. A long lazy lunch can keep your blood alcohol above 0.05 for hours after your last drink, and no app, chart or rule of thumb — including ours — can tell you you're safe to drive. Line up a lift, a rideshare or a sober driver before you start, or make a night of it and stay put.

The trap people miss is the next morning. Alcohol clears at a fixed rate of about one standard drink an hour, and nothing — coffee, a big breakfast, a cold shower — speeds it up. Finish a big Father's Day lunch in the early evening and you can still be over the limit the next morning. Before you drive anywhere, read our guide to driving the morning after drinking.

Estimates are estimates. BAC calculators — including Drink Buddy's — give a guide based on averages, not a measurement. Your real blood alcohol depends on your body, food, medication and more. If there's any doubt at all, don't drive.

Enjoy the whole day

None of this is about drinking less for its own sake. It's about being good company at the end of the afternoon, remembering the day, and getting home without a story you'd rather forget. Pace it right and you get the best of a backyard BBQ — the food, the mates and a cold one in the sun — without the parts nobody enjoys.

If you'd rather not do the maths between snags, log each drink as you pour it. Seeing "6 standard drinks" on your screen mid-afternoon is the nudge no amount of good intentions can match — the same trick that keeps a long wedding day or a day at the footy under control.

Frequently asked questions

How many standard drinks are in a backyard BBQ session?

More than most people think. A 375ml stubby or can of full-strength beer is about 1.4 standard drinks, and a generous home pour of wine is often 1.5 to 2. So a relaxed afternoon of four or five drinks can easily be six to eight standard drinks — above the four-a-day ceiling in the NHMRC guidelines.

Can I drive home after a BBQ?

Plan ahead so you don't have to. Your body clears only about one standard drink an hour, so a long lunch can keep you over 0.05 for hours after your last drink. Arrange a lift, a rideshare or a sober driver before you start — nothing but time lowers your BAC.

Why does a BBQ sneak up on you?

There's no last drinks and no queue to slow you down. Drinks are self-serve, the esky is always close, someone keeps topping up your glass, and it runs for hours. Casual all-day drinking with no cues is how a quiet catch-up becomes a big session.

How do I pace myself at an all-day BBQ?

Eat properly while you drink, aim for no more than about one standard drink an hour, and alternate every drink with water. Pour your own so you know what you've had, choose mid-strength or light beer, and set a limit before you arrive.

Does drinking in the sun affect you more?

It can feel that way. Alcohol is a diuretic and a warm day makes you sweat, so you dehydrate faster and drink quicker. Dehydration doesn't raise your blood alcohol, but it does make a hangover worse. Drink water alongside and sit in the shade where you can.
