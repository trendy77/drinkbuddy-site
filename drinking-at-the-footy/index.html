How to pace your drinks at the footy

Finals season is here, full-strength beer is back at most major grounds, and a day at the footy can quietly turn into one of the bigger drinking sessions of your year. Nobody sets out to overdo it — the game is just long, the cups are big, and the queue does the pacing for you. Here's how to enjoy the whole match and still get home the easy way.

Why the footy catches people out

A day game can run four or five hours from the first bounce to the last song, and longer once you add the pub beforehand and the wait for transport after. Drinks arrive in rounds, the cups are bigger than what you'd pour at home, and a hot afternoon in the stands makes you thirsty — so beer goes down faster and feels more like water than alcohol.

Now that full-strength beer has returned to grounds like Perth's Optus Stadium and is being trialled at major venues in New South Wales, it's worth knowing what you're actually holding. A stadium cup is not a stubby, and four of them is not four standard drinks.

The stadium beer maths

An Australian standard drink is 10 grams of pure alcohol — roughly what your body clears in an hour. As the Australian Government's standard drinks guide shows, almost nothing served at the footy equals exactly one:

Game-day serveStandard drinks
Light beer (425ml cup, 2.7%)0.9
Mid-strength beer (425ml cup, 3.5%)1.2
Full-strength beer (425ml cup, 4.8%)1.6
Full-strength pint (570ml, 4.8%)2.2
Can of full-strength (375ml, 4.8%)1.4
Cup of wine (150ml, 13%)1.5
Pre-mix spirit can (375ml, 6%)1.8

So "four beers over the arvo" — if they're full-strength cups — is more like six to seven standard drinks. That's already above the four-drinks-in-a-day ceiling in Australia's NHMRC alcohol guidelines, before the after-match drinks even start. If you want the full rundown by cup and can size, see our guide to standard drinks in a beer.

The sun makes it worse. Alcohol is a diuretic and a hot day in the stands makes you sweat, so a day game dehydrates you faster than a night out. You feel the effects sooner and reach for the next cold one quicker. healthdirect's advice on dehydration is worth a look before a scorcher.

A game-day pacing plan

Before the first bounce

  • Eat a proper meal. Food in your stomach slows how fast alcohol is absorbed, which flattens the early spike. A pie at quarter-time is not a substitute for lunch.
  • Sort your trip home now. Deciding how you'll get home before the first beer is the single most important call of the day (more below).
  • Pick your number. Choose a realistic total for the day and stick to it. A limit set at 1pm survives; one improvised at three-quarter time doesn't.

Through the game

  • One an hour, at most. Your body clears about one standard drink an hour, so this ceiling keeps you roughly level instead of climbing all afternoon. Remember most cups are 1.2–1.6, so "one an hour" means sipping it, not sculling it.
  • Alternate with water. One water or soft drink between every beer roughly halves your count over the day and takes the edge off the next morning. healthdirect's safe-drinking tips put this near the top of the list for a reason.
  • Order mid-strength or light. Switching to a 3.5% mid-strength cup drops each round from about 1.6 standard drinks to 1.2 — and light beer lower still — so you can keep a beer in your hand all day while your count barely moves.
  • Skip the rounds arms race. Shouts are how three mates turn a two-beer afternoon into a six-beer one. Buy your own, or sit a round out — nobody remembers who shouted last.
  • Ease off before the final siren. Switch to water or a soft drink in the last quarter. It gives your body a head start on the trip home and you'll feel far better tonight.
Cheering on the couch? Watching finals at home has its own trap: no queue, no cup size, and the fridge is right there. Pouring your own means the count creeps up unnoticed — pours at home are often 1.5 to 2 standard drinks each. Log every drink so a "quiet few" watching the game doesn't quietly become eight.

Getting home — and the morning after

Plan not to drive. A few big stadium cups can keep your blood alcohol above 0.05 well after the final siren, and no app, chart or rule of thumb — including ours — can tell you you're safe to drive. Public transport is usually the quickest way out of a packed ground anyway; otherwise line up a rideshare or a sober driver before you go in. Random breath testing is common around grounds on game day, especially after finals.

The trap people miss is the next morning. Alcohol clears at a fixed rate of about one standard drink an hour, and nothing — coffee, a big breakfast, a shower — speeds it up. Finish a big Saturday at midnight and you can still be over the limit on Sunday morning. Before you drive anywhere, check our guide to driving the morning after drinking.

Estimates are estimates. BAC calculators — including Drink Buddy's — give a guide based on averages, not a measurement. Your real BAC depends on your body, food, medication and more. If there's any doubt at all, don't drive.

Enjoy the whole game

None of this is about drinking less for its own sake. It's about being awake for the last quarter of a final, remembering the winning goal, and getting home without a story you'd rather forget. Pace it right and you get the best day out — the footy, the mates and the cold one — without the parts nobody enjoys.

If you'd rather not do the maths in the stands, log each cup as you buy it. Seeing "6 standard drinks" on your screen at three-quarter time is the nudge no amount of good intentions can match — the same trick that keeps a long wedding day under control.

Frequently asked questions

How many standard drinks are in a stadium beer?

A 425ml cup of full-strength beer (4.8%) is about 1.6 standard drinks, and a 570ml pint around 2.2. Because the cups are bigger than a stubby, four beers over an afternoon is closer to six or seven standard drinks.

Can I drive home after the footy?

Plan not to. Your body clears only about one standard drink an hour, so a few big cups can keep you over 0.05 long after the siren. Catch public transport, share a rideshare, or arrange a sober driver — and expect random breath testing around grounds.

How do I pace myself at an all-day game?

Eat before and during, aim for no more than about one standard drink an hour, and alternate every beer with water. Ordering mid-strength or light beer is one of the easiest ways to slow down without missing out.

Is mid-strength beer a good idea at the footy?

Yes. A 425ml mid-strength cup (3.5%) is around 1.2 standard drinks versus about 1.6 for full-strength, so your count climbs far more slowly across a long day. Light beer slower still.

Why does beer seem to hit harder at a day game in the sun?

Alcohol is a diuretic and heat makes you sweat, so a hot day in the stands dehydrates you faster. You feel it more and tend to drink quicker. Water alongside your beer and some shade helps on both counts.
